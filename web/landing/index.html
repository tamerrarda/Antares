ANTARES Launch app

Someone will pay for the upside you weren't going to take.

Antares writes a covered call against a pool of XLM once per round and sells it in an auction anyone can bid into. The premium the auction discovers goes to the depositors, whatever the option does next.

You keep the premium. You cap the rally.

Each round the vault sells a call that runs three days, struck 3 % above the market. Below the strike the pool keeps the collateral and the premium. Above it the pool pays the difference out of its own XLM, so a hard rally leaves you holding fewer coins than you started with. That is the trade, stated the way it actually lands.

The price is discovered, not declared.

A descending auction runs for 45 minutes. The premium starts at 2.80 % of notional and falls to 0.55 %, and whoever thinks it is cheap enough signs a transaction. There is no quote engine and no key that names a price. The whole curve is published in the event that opens the round.

Nobody decides how a round ends.

One call closes a round and it takes no authorization at all. It reads the price as it stood at expiry and dispatches on what it finds: settled, annulled, or unresolved. Calling early or late returns the same number, so the outcome is a function of history rather than of who transacted first.

Pause can stop new risk. It cannot trap old risk.

Pause blocks exactly three calls, and all three are ways in. Nine calls keep working while the vault is paused, including every withdrawal, every claim, and closing a round. Past a fixed deadline a round closes without calling the price feed at all, so no oracle state can strand collateral.

Being the other side costs a premium, not collateral.

A buyer never posts the value of the position. They pay the premium up front and owe nothing after the fill, and the payout can never exceed the XLM already backing it. That is what makes taking the other side possible in a market this thin, and whether anyone will is the question this project exists to answer.
