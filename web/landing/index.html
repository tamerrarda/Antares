The premium is the point.

Antares writes covered calls against a vault of XLM and sells them in a descending-price auction. The premium the auction discovers is paid to depositors, whatever the option does next.

An auction, not a quote.

The price starts high and falls until somebody takes it. Nobody sets the premium — the first bidder willing to pay does, and the curve is published in the event that opens the round, so anyone can evaluate it without asking us.

Every round is verifiable.

State can be rebuilt from transaction hashes and events alone and diffed against what the contract reports. That is a test in this repository, not a claim on this page.

Pause can never hold funds.

The entire exit path is unpausable. Closing a round, requesting and claiming a withdrawal, and every bidder claim all work while the vault is paused — so an admin can stop new risk and can never trap what is already there.

No oracle state can trap a round.

Past a fixed deadline the round closes with no oracle call at all. A dead feed refunds the bidder in full; a feed that merely aged out resolves the other way. The two are different facts and the contract keeps them apart.

Nothing here is a yield figure.

This runs on testnet with test XLM. A depositor can win and can also simply keep their capital and the premium — what they cannot do is be told a number we have not measured.
